PUPSIT (Pre-Use Post Sterilization Integrity Testing) is a European regulatory requirement for sterile medicinal products. PUPSIT is a mandatory work step in [final fill filtration](https://www.sartorius.com/en/products/process-filtration/sterile-filtration/filter-transfer-sets/fts-final-filling) (final filling) for biopharmaceutical manufacturers, except when proven unfeasible.

The USFDA) and PIC/S Regulatory Bodies are collaborating with the EMA on another Annex 1 revision. The upcoming revision maintains the requirement for PUPSIT with some minor modifications. The working group revising Annex 1 wants to create a standard that should apply to all Pharma Mfg industries worldwide.

### As a requirement of the most recent updated version of EU GMP Volume 4 – Annex 1 (2022) “Manufacture of Sterile Medicinal Products” and WHO TRS 1044 – Annex 2 “good manufacturing practices for sterile pharmaceutical products”; verification of the integrity of the sterilized filter assembly should be done before use by Pre-use post-sterilization integrity test (PUPSIT) to ensure no damage or loss of integrity occurred during preparation of filter prior to use.

What it means by PUPSIT (The Buzzword), and Why Does it Matter for Biopharma industry?

PUPSIT measures the integrity of the sterilizing filter and all the components—filter housing, connections, and support structures—after installation and sterilization, but before the drug manufacturers use it.

Since 1971, Annex 1 of the European Union’s Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) offers guidance to help the pharmaceutical industry produce safer medicinal drugs. The latest revision emphasises on Quality Risk Management (QRM). With prior versions, it maintains the requirement for PUPSIT.

Regulatory bodies first introduced PUPSIT to address concerns that manufacturers would not detect microscopic flaws in the post-use test. Flaws may arise during the in-line steam sterilization process allowing contaminants to pass through during the filtration process. **The clogging during filtration would result in a passed integrity post-use**.

***This theoretical “filter flaw masking” could have serious consequences for downstream processing, significantly affecting the quality of the final product, and potentially putting patients at risk.***

How to Perform PUPSIT

During PUPSIT, [manufacturers](https://www.sartorius.com/en/products/process-filtration/sterile-filtration/filter-transfer-sets) must maintain sterility on the downstream side and avoid uncontrolled bioburden on the upstream side of the tested filter. That means installing additional components for a filtration setup:

- **Sterilizing hydrophobic filters** — To avoid Bioburden load increase, Install these filters between the Filter integrity tester and the filter under test. Install another filter at the downstream side of the filter under test to evacuate the test gas & to avoid pressure increase.
- **Sterile container, single-use bag, or hydrophilic filter** — Implement these downstream components/accessories to evacuate the wetting liquid.

The integrity of the filter can be determined in two different ways in spite of their merits /demerits

- **Bubble point test** — This test determines the differential pressure (Delta P) required to expel or remove the wetting liquid from the largest pores of the filter under test. This test is very old & conventional since many years of industry practice.
- **Diffusion | forward flow test** — This test measures the gas flow rate through the porous structure at a defined differential pressure. Normally It is the preferred test for single-use setups because it requires lower pressure.

The Hidden Risks which are associated with these tests outlined here :

PUPSIT tests are complex, leaving more room to introduce errors that otherwise would not exist. Undetected errors could affect the efficacy and safety of the final product. The biggest risks are:

- Operator errors (opening or closing the wrong valves at the wrong time), where thorough & regular training is a MUST for the operators who are performing these tests & they should be certified & authorised to carry out these tests considering their complexity of operation.
- Leaks (from additional components like T-pieces and connectors)

While additional risks that should be taken into consideration include even if they occur very rarely :

- Non-integral vent and drain filters on the downstream side
- Non-integral draining bag

As opposed to pre-use, pre-sterilization integrity testing, and post-use integrity testing, the pre-use post-sterilization integrity testing requires:

- Wetting the filter to be tested under ***sterile conditions***
- Evacuating the wetting liquid under ***sterile conditions***
- Adding complex filtration systems with additional components and connections
- Intervening and aseptic handling from the operator

Any errors in these steps could introduce additional contaminants, nullifying any benefit of the PUPSIT process.

The Benefits/advantages:

Those who are in favour of PUPSIT test counter that it is necessary because current controls during filter manufacturing, transport, and installation are not sufficient to protect against minor flaws. At the same time before it's implementation proper Risk assessment & it's mitigation plan must be in place.

**Recent Revision in the draft :**

In the recently revised 2020 draft, insisting on more detail about the materials used in PUPSIT, how to report results, and examples of compliant tests:

The integrity of the sterilised filter assembly should be verified by integrity testing before use, to check for damage and loss of integrity caused by the filter preparation prior to use.

A sterilising grade filter that is used to sterilise a fluid should be subject to a non-destructive integrity test post-use prior to removal of the filter from its housing.

Test results should correlate to the microbial retention capability of the filter established during validation.

Examples of these tests that are used include :

Bubble point test

Diffusive flow test

Water intrusion test or pressure hold test.

It is recognised that pre-use post-sterilisation integrity testing ***(PUPSIT)*** may not always be possible after sterilisation due to process constraints (e.g. the filtration of very small volumes of solution). In these cases, "an alternative approach may have been adopted with proper risk assessment & compliance Must be achieved through appropriate controls to mitigate any risk of non-sterility.”

"There should be written integrity test methods, including acceptance criteria, failure investigation procedures, and justified conditions under which the filter integrity test can be repeated.

Results of the integrity tests (including failed and repeated tests) should be included in the batch record.”

**Conclusion:**

Testing the integrity of sterilized filters before use is not a new regulatory requirement, the term “PUPSIT” was just recently introduced. Some regulatory agencies do not mandate testing the integrity of sterilized filters before use and others do not require the filter under integrity testing to be sterilized (i.e., integrity test done before sterilization).

Although there are risks associated with PUPSIT regarding impacting the aseptic process, it's still more beneficial to perform PUPSIT to eliminate any risk associated with damage or loss of integrity of the filter which will lead to undesired consequences on product safety and sterility.
